Masterpiece

Play your sampled pipe organs on any computer you own. Masterpiece opens Hauptwerk-format and GrandOrgue sample sets, draws each organ's own console, and answers your MIDI keyboards as the builder wired the instrument.

Consoles

As built

Every organ keeps its own console. Masterpiece builds it from the artwork the producer shipped: the drawstops, the keyboards, the shoes and the jamb pages, each in its place. You register the organ the way its builder laid it out, and thirty-seven sample sets have been tested so far, from a nine-register Flentrop to Nancy's four manuals.

Each of these is a separately published sample set, five by Piotr Grabowski and fourteen by Augustine’s Virtual Organs. Full credits are on the attribution page.

Memory

Fits anywhere

A 17 GB organ plays in 2.3 GB of memory. A cathedral organ can ask for more memory than your computer has. Masterpiece keeps each sample at the resolution it was recorded in, reads the long release tails from disk while they play, and lets you leave out the stops and microphone perspectives you never use.

Measured on Friesach, a 44-stop set of 17 GB, the same organ needs 21.6 GB as 32-bit float, 16.2 GB at 24-bit, 4.65 GB at 16-bit with its releases streamed, and 2.33 GB in mono. Opening it takes 77 seconds at full size, 25.9 streamed in mono, and 18.9 once its sample cache exists. You decide how much of each release stays in memory, from the first second up to three quarters of it, and the status line tells you if your disk ever falls behind.

Packages

Download, play

Point Masterpiece at the RAR archives a producer sent you, or at a GrandOrgue .orgue package, and it plays the organ from there. Solid and multi-volume archives, RAR 4 and RAR 5, all open directly, and after the first load the sample cache makes the next one as fast as an installed set.

Setup

Your bench

Wiring

Couplers, sub- and super-octaves, reversible pistons, crescendo and alternate ranks all run through the set's own switch network. A key you click with the mouse takes the same route as a note from your MIDI keyboard.

Your keyboards

Each MIDI channel plays a manual, with its own key range, transposition, velocity window, short octaves and contact debounce. Teach Masterpiece a drawstop, piston or shoe by moving the real one, then make the whole setup the starting point for every organ you load.

Combinations

Every organ gets up to a hundred generals, divisionals for each manual and a 999-frame stepper of its own, alongside the pistons it already has. Keep a combination set for Sunday and another for a recital, and open your favourite organs from thumb pistons.

Voicing

Adjust level and tuning for a whole rank or a single pipe; the two corrections add together. Two complete voicings, A and B, let you hear a change against the original with one click.

Loading

Resident format, mono folding, sample rate, release streaming, the sample cache and a memory limit sit on one page, together with the stops, perspectives and ranks to load. Changes take effect at once, and you can save them for one organ or for all of them.

Routing

Send each rank to one of up to eight output pairs, so the Pedal can come from its own cabinet. Every rank starts on the first pair, and the pairs sum together when you listen in stereo.

Engine

Inner workings

Releases

Masterpiece measures how long each key was held and plays the release recorded for that length, crossfaded from the sustain.

Wind

Chests, bellows and valves are solved as one system, so a full chord on a heavy registration lowers the pressure and the pipes follow it in pitch and level.

Swell and tremulant

Each enclosure filters its own pipes as the shades move, and each tremulant modulates the pipes on its chest.

Temperaments

Play in the organ's own temperament, in equal temperament, in one of seven historical ones from Werckmeister III to quarter-comma meantone, or from any Scala file, at the pitch you set.

Polyphony

The engine holds 1536 voices. An experimental vector path, using AVX2, SSE2 or NEON, renders 512 of them three to five times faster.

Practice

Record MIDI with every stop and shoe movement and play it back through another registration, record the audio alongside, and keep time with a metronome mixed into the organ's own output.

GrandOrgue

GrandOrgue .organ files load with their panels, mix levels and labels, converted to the same model as a Hauptwerk-format set.

Hardware

MIDI out lights the drawstops of a physical console, jamb displays run over system exclusive, and Masterpiece can publish a MIDI input of its own on macOS and Linux.

Plugins

Load the organ as a VST3 or LV2 plugin, or an Audio Unit on macOS, to sequence it, render it, or record it with other instruments.

Source

Build it

cmake --preset dev
cmake --build --preset dev

Masterpiece is written in C++20 with JUCE, and CMake fetches every library it needs. Continuous integration builds it for Windows, macOS on Apple silicon and Intel, Linux, Raspberry Pi, Android and iOS. The README lists the packages for each system.
