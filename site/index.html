Two formats. One player.

Hauptwerk-format and GrandOrgue sample sets with their own consoles and wiring, read straight from the packages they ship in.

  • Hauptwerk-format definitions.

    Full, compact and composite XML, with every console page, jamb and console size the set draws.

  • GrandOrgue organs.

    .organ definitions converted as they load, console included.

  • Packed or unpacked archives.

    Both formats from an unpacked folder or straight from what they ship in: RAR 4 and 5, solid, multi-part and multi-volume, and GrandOrgue .orgue packages. WAV and WavPack samples at any rate.

  • Each set's own switch network.

    Couplers, sub- and super-octaves, unison off, reversibles, crescendo and alternate ranks.

A vector-optimised engine. More pipes on every processor.

Hand-written AVX2, SSE2 and NEON kernels for every voice, on by default on every processor that has them.

Faster than real time, virtuoso test

  1. 512 voices on an Intel i7, against the same engine without vector code; 1.7–3.3× with SSE2, about 3× with NEON on a Raspberry Pi 4.
  2. Friesach, 24-bit samples: 16.2 GB held in memory, 7.0 GB with release tails streamed. See the table below.
  3. Lemmer on an eight-core i7-9700, 16-bit samples, streamed releases: each audio block computed in 5.4% of its own length on average, and the slowest 1% still 10× faster than real time. The virtuoso test: 1,518 notes in 33 seconds, up to 1,300 notes a second.
  4. Release tail cutoff: tails end once they fall below −90 dB, inaudible in every test, which frees their voices for the notes being played. At −80 dB, the virtuoso test on a two-core i3 runs with 95% fewer late blocks, and a few release recordings with a late sound in them can end a little early.

    Key action, stop action and blower noises made by Masterpiece.

The full feature list

Your keyboards and pedals.

A channel per manual, key range, transposition, velocity window, short octaves and debounce; MIDI learn for every control, drawstop lamps and jamb displays.

Generals, divisionals and stepper.

Up to 100 generals, divisionals per manual, general cancel and a 999-frame sequencer, in named combination sets.

The whole organ on one panel.

Stops grouped by division, for sets drawn across many jambs, any page in its own window, and automatic portrait layouts.

Memory and loading choices.

24 or 16 bits, mono folding, streamed release tails, a sample cache and a memory limit, per organ or as defaults.

Stops, ranks and perspectives.

Any stop, rank or microphone perspective left out of a load, for the largest sets on smaller computers.

Temperaments, pitch and transposer.

The organ's own tuning, nine historical temperaments, Scala files, any pitch for A and ±12 semitones.

Voicing per rank and per pipe.

Level and tuning for every rank and pipe, with two voicing sets for A/B comparison.

A room for sets recorded dry.

Convolution reverb with an impulse response of your choice and its wet level, kept with each organ.

MIDI and audio recording.

Performances with every stop and shoe movement, replayable under another registration; audio takes and a metronome.

Questions organists ask.

Is Masterpiece free?

Yes. Masterpiece is free and open source under the GPL-3.0 licence, with every feature included and nothing to register.

Which sample sets does it play?

Hauptwerk-format sets, from full, compact and composite definitions, and GrandOrgue organs, from .organ definitions and .orgue packages. Samples are WAV or WavPack at any rate; sets with encrypted samples stay with their publisher's own player.

Do the sets need unpacking first?

Masterpiece opens a set from its unpacked folder or straight from its package, solid, multi-part and multi-volume RAR sets included.

Does it run on a Raspberry Pi?

Yes, on Raspberry Pi OS in 64-bit and 32-bit, from a .deb package, with the NEON vector engine.

How much memory does a large organ need?

That depends on the loading settings. Friesach, 17 GB on disk, takes 21.6 GB as 32-bit float, 7.0 GB at 24 bits with streamed release tails, and 2.33 GB at 16 bits in mono. Stops and perspectives can also be left out.

Can it play my console?

Yes: a MIDI channel per manual, any keyboard or pedalboard, and MIDI learn for every stop, piston and swell shoe, consoles that send SysEx included.

Can I play with a single keyboard?

Split mode divides one keyboard into up to three zones, each on a manual or the pedal, with a layered second manual and the pedal from the lowest key held.

Is there a plugin version?

VST3, LV2 and AU plugins come beside the standalone program, for playing the organ inside a digital audio workstation.

Which systems does it run on?

Windows (x64), macOS (Apple silicon and Intel), Linux (Flatpak, .deb and tar.gz), Raspberry Pi, Android 8 and later, and iPhone and iPad with iOS 15 and later, sideloaded.
